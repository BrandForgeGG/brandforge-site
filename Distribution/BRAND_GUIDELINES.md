# BrandForge Brand Guidelines

## Brand name

BrandForge

## Tagline

Describe it. Humans build it.

## Positioning

BrandForge turns one conversation into a real project. You describe what you want to build, AI structures it, and a vetted human team designs, builds and ships it — with escrow protecting your money.

## Color palette

| Name | Hex | Usage |
|------|-----|-------|
| Ink | #14171a | Headings, body text, primary surfaces |
| Ember | #e8571e | CTAs, accents, alerts, interactive states |
| Copper | #b8763b | Secondary accents, icons, borders |
| Trust | #5aa578 | Success states, verification badges, active indicators |
| Paper | #ece7de | Backgrounds, cards, input surfaces |

### Usage rules

- Use Ink for all primary text. Never go lighter than #14171a on a Paper background.
- Ember is the only color used for call-to-action buttons and links. One Ember element per screen section.
- Copper supports Ember but never competes with it. Use Copper for decorative elements and secondary icons.
- Trust appears only for verified actions: delivered work, escrow release, confirmed identity.
- Paper is the default background. Never use pure white. Paper keeps the surface warm.

## Typography

- **Headlines**: Serif. Use a clean serif face with strong letterforms. Headlines carry authority and warmth at the same time.
- **Body**: Sans-serif. Tight set, comfortable measure. The body should disappear into the content.
- **Code / technical bits**: Monospace. Only when showing actual product output or commands.

### Sizing

- H1: 48px
- H2: 36px
- H3: 24px
- Body: 16px
- Small: 14px
- Caption: 12px

### Rules

- Never mix more than one serif and one sans family in a single piece.
- Body text never goes below 14px.
- Line height: 1.6 for body, 1.2 for headlines.

## Logo

The BrandForge mark is the **"BF"** logotype. It is a bold, compact wordmark. The letters are set in the brand's serif face, rendered in Ink on Paper, or Ember on Ink depending on context.

### Usage rules

- Always use the official BF mark. Do not create alternate versions.
- Clear space around the logo equals the height of the "B" on all sides.
- On dark backgrounds, use the Ember version of the mark.
- On light backgrounds, use the Ink version.
- Never stretch, rotate, or recolor the mark outside the palette above.
- The mark is never accompanied by a tagline in the same sentence. The tagline lives separately, below or beside the mark.

## Tone of voice

BrandForge speaks sharp and warm. It sounds like a founder talking to a colleague, not a form filling itself out.

### Core traits

- **Direct.** Say what you mean. No hedging, no "we believe." State the fact.
- **Warm but not cute.** This is not a chatbot. It is not a greeting card. It is a builder talking to a builder.
- **Specific.** Name the thing. Don't say "powerful features." Say "one message, a structured project, and a team that ships it."
- **Human.** Write like someone who types with their hands and ships on Fridays.
- **Short.** Cut every word that does not carry weight. One sentence can do the work of three.

### What to avoid

- No corporate superlatives. "Best," "revolutionary," "industry-leading" — none of it.
- No filler. "We are excited to announce" gets cut to the announcement itself.
- No passive constructions. The team ships it. You describe it. Not "it was built by us."
- No em dashes. Ever.
- No rhetorical questions that answer themselves.

## Do's and don'ts

### Do

- Use the tagline in the first line of any external introduction.
- Name the outcome before the feature. The person cares about what they get, not what the code does.
- Keep paragraphs to three sentences or fewer.
- Lead with the human, then the tool.
- Use Ember on Paper for all primary actions in design files.
- Quote real user outcomes when you have them.

### Don't

- Don't use AI jargon. No "leverage," "synergy," "ecosystem."
- Don't write in the passive voice. Find the actor.
- Don't use more than two colors in a single marketing asset.
- Don't pair the logo with more than one line of tagline.
- Don't use stock photos of people pointing at screens.
- Don't use exclamation points. Ever.
- Don't describe features without naming the result.
- Don't use Comic Sans, Futura, or any font not in the typography guide.
